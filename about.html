One smith, one forge, one town.

Ashwood Forge started in a rented garage on the edge of Thornbury in 2016, with a coal forge, a borrowed anvil and a stack of library books on heat-treating steel.

"I wanted to make the kind of tool that gets handed down instead of thrown out."

Ten years on, it's still a workshop of one smith and one apprentice, working in the same corner of town — just with a proper anvil now.

How a commission is made

The same four stages, whether it's a paring knife or a gate.

  1. Select steel & design

    We talk through what the piece needs to do, then choose the steel and sketch the profile together.

  2. Forge & shape

    The steel is heated and hammered into rough shape while it's still workable, not ground from a flat bar.

  3. Heat-treat & temper

    Hardened in the forge, then tempered in the kitchen oven to the exact hardness the piece calls for.

  4. Finish & fit

    Hand-sanded, edge honed, and handle or hardware fitted before it ever leaves the bench.

What guides the work

Repairable over disposable

Every piece is built so it can be resharpened, rehandled or reheated rather than replaced.

Small batches, honestly priced

We quote for the hours a piece actually takes, not what a factory version would cost.

Teaching what we know

Classes run most weekends, because a workshop that keeps its skills to itself doesn't help anyone.
